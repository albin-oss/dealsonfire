# DOF Merchant Experience — MERCHANT_DAILY_RHYTHM

**Status:** For Founder approval (design only) · v1.0 · 2026-07-27
The shape of a maker's day with DOF in it — designed so the platform fits around the craft, never the reverse. A maker's day belongs at the bench; DOF earns minutes, not hours.

---

## 1. The morning visit (90 seconds, built spine)
Open the workspace → **what happened** (recent activity: fires, follows, overnight orders) → **what's the one thing** (the hero: pack the blanket / answer the spark moment / nothing today) → leave. The "Say good morning" card already models this. Rhythm law: **the workspace must be leavable in under two minutes with complete peace of mind** — if anything needed attention, it was THE one thing; if nothing appears, nothing is waiting. No unread counts accumulating dread; no feed to clear.

## 2. Order days (C5/C6)
An order arrives at `confirmed` (certainty law) → one Pulse task with the promise date visible ("pack by Thursday to keep Friday's promise") → pack flow ends with the two one-touch warmths (parcel photo, done; the case closes) → the day's craft continues. Multiple orders group into the day's bench list, never an inbox: **tasks are physical work items, so they're phrased as physical work** ("3 parcels to pack"), not tickets.

## 3. The posting rhythm (the maker decides)
Sparks happen when something real happened — a batch finished, a dye lot surprised, a shelf restocked. The composer's evolution under the Workshop Wait: the audience line names who's waiting. **No prompts to post on a schedule, ever.** The playbook may teach the craft-fair habit ("tell the street what's on the bench this week"); the software never schedules the maker.

## 4. Waiting states (the merchant's side of anticipation)
Merchants wait too: for the first order, for a payout, for a buyer's parcel to arrive. Each waiting state names the next real event and its expected timing ("payout scheduled Friday" · "in transit — usually 2 days") — the same narrated-waiting law as the buyer side. Silence with a clock beats spinners with none.

## 5. Evening / closing the day
No end-of-day ritual is imposed (a ritual demanded is a chore). But the workspace at evening naturally shows the day's small ledger — activity newest-first reads as "what today brought." Closing the laptop mid-anything is safe by law (drafts survive; nothing times out).

## 6. Days off, seasons, and the quiet stall
The market vendor's right: the stall closes Sundays, and August happens.
- **No penalty mechanics exist to need disabling** — chronological street, no streaks, no decay. A week away costs a week of quiet, nothing more.
- The buyer side protects the resting maker (presence line falls back to opened-date; storefront never displays staleness shaming).
- **Named seam (gated, not scheduled):** a "the shop is resting" notice — merchant-set, buyer-visible, honest ("back in September"). Composition over Brand Kit + storefront; requires founder approval when merchants actually ask (watch the friction log for vacation anxiety).

## 7. Notification philosophy (binding on the C7 seam)
Three classes only: **money & promises** (order confirmed, promise at risk, dispute deadline — delivered promptly; dispute deadlines are the one sanctioned urgency) · **milestones** (Moment Ledger — delivered gently, batched to the morning) · **everything else** (in the workspace when they next visit; never pushed). The default is calm: a DOF merchant's phone buzzes only when a real promise or real money moved. No marketing notifications from platform to merchant, ever.

## 8. The rhythm test
A maker with a stall at Saturday's market, four products, and a day job must be able to run their DOF shop **entirely in the morning visit + order days** and feel fully present on the street. If any future feature requires daily tending to avoid decay, it violates this document.
