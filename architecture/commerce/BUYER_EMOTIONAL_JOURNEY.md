# DOF Buyer Experience — BUYER_EMOTIONAL_JOURNEY

**Status:** For Founder approval (design only) · v1.0 · 2026-07-27
The BUYER_JOURNEY (trust) walked again, this time asking only: *how should this feel, and what remains tomorrow — and next year?* Same ten steps; the fear column is done; this is the desire column.

---

**Before — discovery & consideration**

- **The street** should feel like *market morning*: many voices, today's word, no barker. Emotion: browsing-pleasure without acquisition-pressure. Already true by law (chronological, no urgency); this layer adds nothing here — restraint is the design.
- **The product page** should feel like *stopping at a stall*: the thing in your hands, the maker a glance away, their week's work visible ("From the maker" strip). The buyer should feel they are interrupting real work in the best way. Memory-tomorrow: "the knitter who posted about dye lots."
- **Choosing a variant** should feel like *asking "do you have it in green?"* — the chips carry the maker's own option words (already true: merchant-language law). Never a SKU picker.

**During — commitment & the wait**

- **Add to cart** should feel like *setting it aside on the stall table* — reserved-ish, warm, unfinal. The cart page's per-store sections keep it feeling like *stalls*, not a warehouse basket. (Built; C1.)
- **Checkout (C3, governed here)** should feel like *the moment of handing over money at the stall*: brief, direct, dignified. No upsells at the till — the single most anti-stall pattern in ecommerce. Paying should take less emotional time than choosing did.
- **The confirmation** is *the letter* (approved design): the first beat of anticipation. It names the next real event ("Rosa usually starts within 2 days") — anticipation needs a *next thing to picture*, not a tracking number.
- **The wait** is the first chapter (EMOTIONAL_MODEL §3): the order timeline interleaves the maker's sparks from the wait window. Checking the order page should feel like *walking past the workshop window*, not refreshing logistics. Memory-tomorrow: "she's winding the skeins today." **This is the phase DOF wins outright.**

**After — arrival, ownership, return**

- **Arrival** should feel like *handover*: the timeline's final entry says who kept the promise, by how much, and — where the merchant chose to — shows the parcel photo from pack-time (§ the C6 seam in DOF_SIGNATURE_EXPERIENCES). The delivered box was *sent by hands*, and the last thing the buyer saw was those hands closing it.
- **Ownership** should feel like *provenance*: the order page quietly becomes **the biography of the thing** — the letter, the wait-window sparks, the promise kept, the maker's sign-off, preserved. A year later, "who made your blanket?" has an answer with dates in it. Order history stops being an accounting list and becomes a shelf of stories. Memory-next-year: this page.
- **The return visit** should feel like *"back again?"*: Home already knows (follows, corner, new-since-last-visit); C5+ adds "you've bought from Rosa before" where the buyer earned it. Regularhood: recognized because you *did things*, never because you were tracked.

---

## The feeling checkpoints (the test C3+ ships against)

1. After confirmation, can the buyer *picture the next real event*? (anticipation exists)
2. During the wait, does visiting the order page ever feel like walking past the workshop? (narrative exists)
3. On arrival day, does the buyer know a person kept a promise? (handover exists)
4. A year later, does the thing still have its story? (provenance exists)
5. On the second visit, does it feel like returning somewhere? (regularhood exists)

A commerce increment that ships "functionally complete" but fails its checkpoint is incomplete — these join the Experience Review checklist for C3–C9.
