# DOF Buyer Experience — MERCHANT_TO_BUYER_CONNECTION

**Status:** For Founder approval (design only) · v1.0 · 2026-07-27
The channel design: how a maker's humanity reaches a buyer at each phase — without demanding performance from merchants (L5) and without AI ever speaking as a human (R-class law: words to buyers are merchant-signed, forever).

---

## 1. The connection inventory (every touch, its carrier, its cost to the merchant)

| Phase | Connection | Carrier | Merchant effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | the voice (story, promise, tagline) | Brand Kit (exists) | done once in Ignite |
| Consideration | the week's work | sparks (exist) | the habit they already have |
| Commitment | the promise, in their words | promise line at DP-3 (exists) | none — reused |
| Confirmation | **the sign-off** | the letter closes with the merchant's promise line as signature; optionally their own standing thank-you sentence (§2) | one sentence, once |
| The wait | the workshop window | wait-window sparks interleaved in the timeline (read-time join; no new content) | none |
| Dispatch | **the parcel photo** (§3) | optional photo at the C6 pack/dispatch flow, via the existing Media Port | ~10 seconds, optional |
| Arrival | the kept promise, attributed | timeline final entry names the maker, not the carrier | none |
| Afterward | the biography page | preserved automatically | none |

The column that matters is the last one: **every connection is either already-made words, an existing habit, or a one-touch option.** A connection design that assigns merchants homework produces guilty merchants and dead channels — the stall works because the maker is *already there*, not performing extra.

## 2. The standing sign-off (C5 seam, merchant-owned)
One optional sentence, set once in the Store identity editor: *"Thank you — every blanket leaves with a note about its wool."* Appears under the letter's next-event line, above the promise-line signature. Their words, written once, honestly reused (the letter says nothing false: a standing sentence is a standing sentiment — like the sign at the stall). **Not** per-order forced notes: demanded gratitude reads as printed gratitude. If a merchant wants per-order words someday, that is the messaging capability — separate, gated, unscheduled.

## 3. The parcel photo (C6 seam, the highest-value ten seconds in the design)
At pack/dispatch, the flow offers — never requires — one photo: *"Show them their parcel?"* It lands in the buyer's timeline at the "packed" event and in the biography. This is the stall's wrapping-paper moment translated exactly: care made visible, zero words needed, real by construction (it is *their* parcel). Ships with the C6 dispatch flow via the existing Media Port; founder-gated at C6 like all observable additions.

## 4. What connection is NOT
No buyer→merchant free-text channel in this layer (messaging is its own future capability with its own safety design). No merchant photos of buyers, no "meet your buyer" — the connection is deliberately **asymmetric**: the maker is public, the buyer is private. No response-time expectations implied anywhere until a channel exists to honor them.

## 5. Failure honesty in connection
When the merchant goes quiet mid-wait (life happens), the platform carries the channel: true case states, promise-at-risk disclosure, the automatic-refund backstop. The buyer should never experience *both* a silent maker and a silent platform. The platform's voice is calm, factual, and never impersonates warmth it didn't earn (EMOTIONAL_MODEL §5).
