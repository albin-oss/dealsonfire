# DOF Buyer Experience — BUYER_JOURNEY

**Status:** For Founder approval (design only) · v1.0 · 2026-07-27
The complete path of one first-time buyer, with the fear-state and the answering evidence at every step. Persona used for honesty: someone who clicked a shared link to a €45 blanket from a shop they've never heard of, on a platform they've never heard of.

---

## Step 0 — The link unfurls (before DOF is even open)
**State:** idle curiosity; zero trust. **What they see:** the product title, the maker's name, a real photo (the SEO builders already carry this). **Design intent:** the unfurl promises a *person and a thing*, not a SaaS ("Lavender Blanket — Rosa Knits"), so the first impression is already the maker.

## Step 1 — Landing on the product page
**Fear peak: F1 (is this real?).** The first five seconds decide everything.
**Answers on screen (INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE §2):** the merchant's brand dress (not a template default) · the product photo as hero · **the presence line** — "Rosa Knits · 23 people follow · posted 2 days ago" with the latest spark one tap away · the maker's own description voice.
**Anti-signal audit:** no dead space that smells generated; no lorem-like fallback copy anywhere on the path (empty states speak as DOF, never as absence).

## Step 2 — The look-around (30–90 seconds)
**Fear: F3 (will it match the photos?), residual F1.** The buyer pokes: more photos, the shelf, the storefront, maybe the street.
**Answers:** "More from Rosa Knits" (already built) · the storefront's Latest-from strip with timestamps (built) · the story + promise block in Rosa's words (built) · "New this week" honesty on fresh items (built). The look-around is DOF's home turf — it is exactly what the street already does well.

## Step 3 — The pause at the price
**Fear peak: F2 + F5 (will it arrive? is €45 fair?).** This is where most unknown-merchant purchases die.
**Answers, placed AT the price (DECISION_POINTS §2):** delivery expectation in plain words ("usually ships in 2–4 days" once case history exists; the merchant-set promise date before that) · the outcome line once it exists ("41 of 43 orders arrived on time") · below threshold: "Opened this March — first orders will build this record here."

## Step 4 — The hover over the button
**Fear peak: F4 (if this goes wrong, am I stranded?).** The final gate.
**Answer — the keystone, directly under the actions:** *"You're charged at purchase. Rosa isn't paid until your order ships — and if it doesn't ship, your refund is automatic."* One sentence, platform-voiced, identical on every store (L2). Nothing else new: the promise line ("✓ If something isn't right, we make it right") already sits here in the merchant's voice — merchant warmth above, platform structure below.

## Step 5 — Add to cart (built, C1)
**State shift: fear → possession.** The thing is *theirs-ish*. The cart keeps every honesty promise (re-quotes, availability, cross-device).
**One addition from this layer:** the cart line carries the store's presence line, so returning to the cart tomorrow re-encounters a living maker, not a row in a table.

## Step 6 — Checkout (C3 — designed there, governed here)
**Fears re-spike briefly at payment entry.** This layer's laws bind C3: guest-first (no wall) · the keystone sentence beside the pay button · the promise date shown as a *promise*, in the merchant's voice, before payment · every failure honest and reversible-feeling (nothing charged, nothing lost).

## Step 7 — The confirmation (C3/C5)
**State: post-decision anxiety ("did I just do something stupid?").** The most under-designed moment in commerce.
**Design:** the confirmation is a *letter, not a receipt* — "It's on its way to being yours. Rosa usually ships within 3 days; we'll tell you the moment it does." Merchant voice for warmth, platform voice for the guarantee, order number quietly present. SIGNATURE_MOMENTS §3.

## Step 8 — The wait (C5/C6)
**Fear: slow-burn F2.** Silence reads as abandonment after ~48h.
**Design:** the buyer timeline shows true case states with time-language ("packed yesterday") · promise-at-risk triggers proactive disclosure BEFORE the buyer worries (the Recovery Journey — merchant-signed, R2) · the no-ship aging path ends in the automatic refund the keystone promised.

## Step 9 — Arrival
**State: relief → judgment.** The blanket exists; is it good?
**Design:** delivery confirmation closes the promise loop as a *recorded fact* (the promise-kept ledger grows — no review begging, no stars). One quiet prompt, once: "Did everything arrive as promised?" — a yes/no promise-check feeding the Class-1 record, with a free-text note to the merchant (private, not a public review). Public buyer words remain out of scope (rejected as a purchasable signal).

## Step 10 — The return visit
**State: trust earned; relationship begins.** Their corner remembers the maker; Home shows Rosa's next spark with "you follow" context; (C5+) the shop page can say "you've bought here before."
**Design intent:** the second purchase should feel like *returning*, not re-deciding — repeat-buyer facts eventually become other buyers' Class-1 evidence, closing the flywheel.

---

## The journey in one line
Curiosity → *a person is here* → *the thing is real* → *it will arrive* → *I'm protected either way* → possession → a kept promise, witnessed and recorded → the next buyer's evidence.
