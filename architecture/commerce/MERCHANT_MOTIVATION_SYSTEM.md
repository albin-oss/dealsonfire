# DOF Merchant Experience — MERCHANT_MOTIVATION_SYSTEM

**Status:** For Founder approval (design only) · v1.0 · 2026-07-27
How DOF keeps a maker making — built exclusively from real work accomplished, per the founder's law. This is the anti-gamification system: it has no points because it has receipts.

---

## 2. The design rule that holds it together
**Every motivational sentence must contain a fact the merchant can verify by clicking it.** "Your words are working" is permitted only because "2 new people followed this week" sits in the same sentence. Motivation without receipts is manipulation with better manners — banned.

## 3. Discouragement design (the half nobody builds)
Makers quit in the valleys, not the peaks. The valley mechanics:
- **The quiet week:** momentum sentence degrades gracefully — never "engagement is down 40%," instead the mentor's frame: "a quiet week — one honest line about what's on the bench usually wakes the street up." Evidence-bearing, shame-free, actionable.
- **The first bad outcome** (late shipment, return, dispute): the record shows it plainly (honesty law) AND the coaching frame arrives in the same breath: "one late shipment on the record — your last five were on time; the record heals as you keep promises." Plus the fairness structure: recency is visible, so a bad month is visibly a *month*, not a scar.
- **The abandoned draft:** everything-survives law (drafts persist) + the workspace never nags about them. An unfinished product idea waits like a sketch on the bench — present, silent.
- **The failure messages:** every error already follows "nothing was lost" (built law). Extension: failure copy must never assign blame to the merchant ("that didn't take" — not "you entered an invalid…").

## 4. What we deliberately do NOT motivate
Posting frequency (cadence targets are the burnout engine) · listing volume ("add more products!" — a curated shelf of four things is a valid shop, and the street's aesthetic depends on it) · discounting (deals are the merchant's tool, never the platform's demand) · growth for its own sake (a two-order-a-week shop that keeps every promise is a *successful shop* on DOF, and every surface must agree).

## 5. The six-month test, mechanically
At six months a merchant's workspace contains: their moments (first sale, first kept promise, first return buyer — visitable), their record (counts they earned), their street presence (followers, corner-keepers), and tomorrow's one thing. Nothing in it is a chart of them; all of it is a mirror of their work. That is the difference between "I manage a store" (software about you) and "I have a workshop" (a place made of what you did).
