# DOF Buyer Experience — BUYER_EMOTIONAL_MODEL

**Status:** For Founder approval (design only; everything approved stays frozen) · v1.0 · 2026-07-27
**Relationship to the Trust Model:** trust removes fear; this model creates desire. Same laws (real events only, no gamification, no fake delight, nothing ranks), different axis.

---

## 1. What the market stall knows that ecommerce forgot

Study set: farmers markets, craft fairs, family bookstores, coffee shops, independent makers. Strip away nostalgia and five *mechanisms* remain — each producing a specific emotion ecommerce lost:

| Stall mechanism | Emotion produced | What ecommerce did instead |
|---|---|---|
| You watch hands making the thing | **witness** — "I saw it become" | hid production behind stock photos |
| The maker hands it to you | **handover** — the thing arrives *from someone* | a courier leaves a box |
| "Back again? The usual?" | **regularhood** — being known | surveillance-flavored "recommended for you" |
| The story told at the stall ("this wool is from the valley") | **provenance** — the thing carries a story | bullet-point spec lists |
| The wait while they wrap it, the paper, the string | **care made visible** | "arrives tomorrow" as the only virtue |

**The translation law:** digitize the *mechanism*, never the aesthetic. No woodgrain textures, no handwritten fonts, no fake twine. A spark timestamped yesterday IS watching hands work; forced rustic styling is a costume.

## 2. The emotional arc of one purchase

Desire is not one feeling; it's a sequence, and each phase has one dominant emotion the design must serve — and one counterfeit it must refuse:

| Phase | Dominant emotion to serve | Counterfeit to refuse |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | **curiosity** — a person worth watching | urgency ("selling fast!") |
| Consideration | **wanting this-from-them** (the maker is part of the object's value) | generic want (any blanket would do) |
| Commitment | **quiet confidence** (trust layer's job) | transactional triumph ("great deal!") |
| **The wait** | **anticipation** — the emotion incumbents amputate | impatience management ("track your package!") |
| Arrival | **handover** — receiving, not just getting | logistics closure ("delivered.") |
| Ownership | **provenance** — the thing keeps its story | buyer's amnesia (order #4521 in a list) |
| Return | **regularhood** — being a regular at a stall | retention mechanics (win-back coupons) |

## 3. The central design claim: the wait is the product's first chapter

Amazon optimizes the wait toward zero because for interchangeable goods waiting is pure cost. For a made thing, **the wait is when the thing is being made** — the one phase where "buying from a person" is literally, physically true. DOF is the only platform whose architecture already narrates it: the maker's sparks are a chronological workshop stream, and an order gives the buyer a *stake* in that stream.

So: the order timeline doesn't just show case states — it interleaves **the maker's public sparks published during the buyer's wait**. "While your blanket was on the needles, Rosa posted: *'the dye lot came out deeper than usual and honestly? We love it.'*" No new content demanded of merchants (L5 — sparks already exist); no new capability (a read-time join of two existing streams); and the buyer's wait becomes serialized anticipation instead of tracking-number anxiety.

## 4. Emotions deliberately not engineered

**Guilt** ("support small businesses!") — charity framing shrinks makers; DOF sells *better things from visible hands*, not pity. **Urgency/FOMO** — banned by the trust layer, doubly banned here; anticipation is slow desire, urgency is its junk-food substitute. **Parasocial intimacy** — the maker is a person with a shop, not a follower-farming influencer; the design never pressures merchants to perform private life (behind-the-scenes is *work*, not *self*). **Awe at the platform** — DOF is the stall's street, never the show; any moment where the platform upstages the maker is a defect.

## 5. The asymmetry that keeps it honest

Every emotional mechanism here is **a rendering of something real that already happened** (a spark, a pack event, a kept promise, a return visit). The moment DOF generates warmth that no event earned — an auto-"thank you" the merchant never said, a fake "made with love" flourish — the entire model collapses into the greeting-card uncanny valley. Rule: **DOF may frame feelings; only people and events may cause them.** (The R-class law already enforces the hardest case: words to buyers are merchant-signed, forever.)
