# DOF Merchant Experience — THE_DOF_WORKSHOP

**Status:** For Founder approval (design only) · v1.0 · 2026-07-27
The definition document for the merchant side, twin to THE_DOF_FEELING: what the workspace IS, named precisely enough to test every screen against.

---

## 1. The name and the metaphor

**The workspace is a workshop with a door onto the street.**

- **Front of house** (the storefront, the street): the merchant's public stall — their brand, their voice, buyers walking past.
- **The bench** (products, sparks, deals): where things are made and placed on the shelf.
- **The back wall** (the record, moments, the ladder): what the work has amounted to — pinned where the maker can see it, invisible to no one who matters, gamed by no one at all.
- **The door** (View live, share, the presence line): always one step between bench and street — the storefront preview law ("what you see here IS what the street sees") is this door's glass.

The test for every merchant screen, from the founder's brief, operationalized: **"does this feel like software, or like owning a workshop?"** Concretely — software *reports on you* (charts, statuses, admin nouns); a workshop *is made of your work* (things, words, promises, moments). Every screen must be composed of the merchant's own artifacts, with platform chrome as shelving, never as subject.

## 3. The vocabulary (merchant side, binding)
Extends the frozen merchant-language law: **uses** — shelf, bench, parcel, promise, the street, your record, people (never traffic), waiting (never pending). **Never** — inventory management, order processing, fulfillment pipeline, customer acquisition, performance metrics, dashboard. Lint-level copy review at each increment (the C3–C9 checklist).

## 4. The two chairs
A workshop has a maker's chair and a visitor's chair. Every surface is designed from the maker's chair (what do I want to make/keep today?) — but each carries one glance-line from the visitor's chair (the presence line, the preview, "buyers will see this as…"). The confidence system's rule that merchants see their record before buyers do is this principle in law: **no merchant is ever surprised by their own shop window.**

## 5. What the workshop refuses to become
An analytics console (facts live in sentences; the moment DOF ships a chart of the merchant to the merchant, this document has been violated) · a task manager (one thing, not a backlog) · a CRM (buyers are people who bought, not records to work) · a growth cockpit (no funnels, no conversion language anywhere merchant-visible).
